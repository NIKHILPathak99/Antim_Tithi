Antyeshti Vidhi

The thirteen days that carry the soul onward

Hindu tradition holds that after death the soul remains in a subtle preta form until the rites are complete. Each day of the antyeshti builds that subtle body, releases attachment to the household, and finally unites the departed with the ancestors.

Day 1 — Antim Sanskar at the ghat

Day 1–10 — Dashgatra pind daan

Day 13 — Terahvi and shanti path

Day 1

Antim Sanskar

Cremation at the ghat. The chief mourner performs mukhagni and kapal kriya, and the family observes ashaucha from this moment.

Day 2

Sthal Shuddhi

The cremation site is cleansed with water and milk. A lamp and water pot are kept at the doorway for the departed soul.

Day 3–4

Asthi Sanchayan

The asthi are collected in a kalash and taken for immersion in the Ganga or another sacred river.

Day 1–10

Dashgatra Vidhi

One pind is offered each day. Each offering forms a limb of the subtle body — head, neck, shoulders, senses, and finally hunger and thirst.Cremation at the ghat. The chief mourner performs mukhagni and kapal kriya, and the family observes ashaucha from this moment.

Day 10

Dashmi Snan

The family shaves and bathes, the house is cleaned, and the heaviest phase of impurity ends.

Day 11

Ekadashah Shraddha

Havan, vrishotsarga and daan of shayya, vastra and grain. Eleven or more brahmins are fed.

Day 12

Sapindikaran

The preta pind is divided and merged into the pind of the three preceding generations — the soul becomes a Pitru.

Day 13

Terahvi & Shanti Path

Shanti path, Garuda Purana recital and a community satvik bhoj. The family returns to daily life.

Month 1

Masik Shraddha

Monthly shraddha is observed for the first year on the same tithi as the day of death..

Year 1

Varshik Shraddha

The first annual shraddha completes the cycle. Thereafter tarpan is offered yearly and in Pitru Paksha.

Who performs the rites

The karta is traditionally the eldest son, but a grandson, brother, nephew, daughter or the closest relative may perform them. In the absence of family, a pandit performs the rites as the ritual son.

Ashaucha, the impurity period

The immediate family observes restrictions on temple worship, festivals and auspicious work for thirteen days, with the strictest observance until the tenth-day snan.

Regional variation

North Indian, Maharashtrian, Bengali and South Indian traditions differ in the day of terahvi, the form of pind and the mantras used. We follow your family's paddhati.
